# Acts of Defiance — Brand Voice Guide

## Personality Hierarchy

1. **Accessible educator** (lead) — Meet people where they are. No assumed knowledge. Build understanding without condescension. If someone doesn't know who Frantz Fanon is, that's an opportunity, not a failure.
2. **Passionate storyteller** — History is made of people, not abstractions. Lead with narrative, emotion, lived experience. Make the reader feel why this matters.
3. **Scholarly backbone** — Rigorous sourcing, accurate history, honest about complexity. The authority comes from the work, not the voice.
4. **Sharp when it counts** — Don't be afraid to name injustice directly. But sharpness is a spice, not the main course. Earned through the preceding rigor, not used as a default posture.

## Site Voice

The site's own voice — navigation, taglines, category descriptions, empty states, about page — is **declarative with warmth**. Short, punchy, almost manifesto-like. Inclusive "we" when addressing the community. Never lecturing, never institutional.

### Examples

| Context | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| Category intro | "Every movement started with someone who refused." | "Explore our collection of movement histories." |
| Empty state | "This story hasn't been told yet." | "No articles found." |
| About | "We believe defiance is an act of hope." | "Acts of Defiance is a publication dedicated to..." |
| Tag page | "Everything we've written about labor rights." | "Articles tagged: labor rights" |
| Article count | "12 acts of defiance" | "12 results" |
| Footer | "The struggle continues." | "Copyright 2026 Acts of Defiance" |

### Voice principles

- Use **"we"** and **"us"** inclusively — the publication and the reader are on the same side
- **Declarative statements** over descriptions — "Defiance is an act of hope" not "This site is about acts of defiance"
- **Present tense** when possible — these stories are alive, not archived
- **Short sentences** for UI copy — every word earns its place
- Let the art and content carry the weight — the site voice frames, it doesn't compete

## Voice Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use "we" and "us" inclusively | Use institutional third-person ("the publication") |
| Name injustice plainly | Hedge or both-sides oppression |
| Explain context without condescension | Assume the reader knows the history |
| Let complexity exist — things can be messy | Oversimplify into heroes and villains |
| Credit sources and movements, not just leaders | Center Great Man narratives |
| Write at high-school reading level | Use academic jargon without explanation |
| Be direct and warm | Be detached or clinical |
| Use active voice | Hide behind passive constructions |

## Article voice

Articles follow the personality hierarchy above. The lead personality shifts by article type:

| Article type | Lead with | Support with |
|---|---|---|
| Historical profile | Passionate storyteller | Scholarly backbone |
| Movement overview | Accessible educator | Passionate storyteller |
| Analysis piece | Scholarly backbone | Sharp when it counts |
| Educational explainer | Accessible educator | Scholarly backbone |

All article types maintain accessibility as a floor — never sacrifice clarity for sophistication.

## Relationship to Content Guide

The content guide (`docs/projects/acts-of-defiance/content-guide.md`) defines article creation capabilities for the dime agents — what types of content to produce, research standards, editorial process, and templates. This voice guide defines the *personality* those articles should embody. The content guide answers "what do we write and how." This answers "who are we when we speak."

Agent prompts should reference both documents: the content guide for structure and standards, this voice guide for tone and personality.
